Date received: 22 Aug 2026

The fields and hedgerows surrounding Gainsthorpe provide important habitats for a variety of species. Birds such as skylarks, yellowhammers, linnets, and owls may use the area for nesting, feeding, or hunting. The construction and operation of a large solar farm could disturb these species, particularly if existing hedgerows, grassland, or field margins are altered.

There could also be effects on mammals including hedgehogs, hares, bats, and foxes. Bats may be affected by changes to foraging routes, while hedgehogs and other ground-dwelling animals could experience habitat fragmentation caused by fencing and associated infrastructure.

The area may also support reptiles, amphibians, and a range of insects, including butterflies, moths, bees, and other pollinators that rely on wildflowers and undisturbed grassland habitats. Construction activity, increased traffic, noise, and changes in land management could reduce the suitability of these habitats.

Gainsthorpe Wildlife Protection Group is concerned that the long-term effects on local wildlife have not been fully understood. Given the importance of protecting biodiversity, a detailed ecological assessment should be undertaken to ensure that any development does not result in a net loss of habitats or negatively affect species that contribute to the natural environment around Gainsthorpe.
